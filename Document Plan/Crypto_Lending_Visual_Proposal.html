01 / The idea

What are we proposing?

A customer pledges some cryptocurrency as security for a loan and receives cash without first selling that asset. LendFoundry would coordinate the loan journey; a lending partner would provide the money; an approved provider would safeguard the pledged asset.

Confirmed: existing LendFoundry loan flowProposed: crypto extensionOpen: lender, customer and state
1 · ApplyCustomer asks for a loan and identifies the asset.
2 · VerifyIdentity, location, fraud and asset risks are checked.
3 · SecureApproved collateral is placed under agreed control.
4 · FundLender sends money only after final checks pass.
5 · ServiceCustomer repays; the asset is monitored and then returned.
Simple example · illustrative

A $10,000 loan backed by $20,000 of Bitcoin

$20,000Bitcoin placed as collateral
$10,000Cash loan to the customer
50%Starting loan-to-value (LTV): loan ÷ collateral value

If the Bitcoin falls to $13,000 and the loan remains $10,000, LTV rises to about 77%. The customer may need to add collateral or repay part of the loan. If risk crosses a contractually defined point, some collateral may be sold. Those trigger levels have not been chosen.

Why customers care

They can access money while retaining exposure to an asset they do not want to sell today.

Why lenders care

The pledged asset provides a source of recovery, provided it can be controlled and sold when needed.

Why LendFoundry fits

Most of the familiar work already exists: application, decisions, documents, funding and servicing. Crypto adds asset controls and continuous monitoring.

02 / Side by side

How is this different from today's LendFoundry loan?

The supplied traditional journey is: Application → soft credit check → citizenship → Plaid identity → program qualification → hard credit check → DTI and income → offer → e-sign → funding. A crypto-backed journey retains the loan stages but adds collateral checks before funding and active risk management afterward.

QuestionCurrent LendFoundry workflowProposed crypto-backed workflow
Who is the customer?Citizenship, Plaid identity and credit information.Identity, supported location, sanctions/fraud checks and optional credit or income checks if the selected product requires them.
Can we offer a loan?Program rules, FICO, income and debt-to-income ratio (DTI).Program rules plus supported asset, available collateral, market value and LTV. Credit/DTI may still apply.
What must happen before funding?Offer accepted, documents signed, final underwriting and bank funding.All of those, plus the pledged asset must be received, screened, securely held and revalued immediately before funding.
What happens after funding?Collect payments and service the loan.Collect payments and monitor the asset's value around the clock; warn, accept top-ups, liquidate when legally and contractually allowed, then return remaining collateral at payoff.
The core difference: traditional lending focuses heavily on whether the borrower can repay. Crypto-backed lending must also prove that the collateral is real, under control, valuable and sellable throughout the loan.
03 / Application flow

The complete loan journey

This diagram follows the same decision-and-exception pattern as the supplied flowchart. A review path pauses the journey. A failed check stops it. Every “pass” moves the application toward funding.

From application to funded loan

LTV means loan amount divided by eligible collateral value. The specific approval, warning and liquidation thresholds are business decisions.

Decision in the diagramChecks behind itIf it cannot pass
Identity + eligibilityIdentity, legal location, sanctions and fraud; credit or income only if the chosen product requires them.Request more evidence, send to a reviewer or decline.
Safe loan amountApproved asset and network, wallet/deposit risk, available amount, market price, LTV and lender limits.Offer less, request more collateral or stop.
Final funding gateSigned terms, confirmed deposit, clear transaction screening, fresh price, final LTV and available lender funds.Hold funding until the issue is resolved.

Before funding

Review and stop branches protect the customer and lender. No cash is released until the signed agreement, asset control, compliance result and fresh valuation all agree.

After funding

The loan becomes active. Payments follow the agreed schedule while price changes and the remaining loan balance are monitored continuously.

After funding: four possible paths

If a provider or price feed fails, new funding and irreversible actions pause under an approved incident rule; safe payments and top-ups should remain available where possible.

If a payment is late: send the required notice and apply the approved cure/default process. If the price buffer is breached: request more collateral or repayment, then use the agreed sale process if the trigger is met. If a transfer fails: keep the loan pending, reconcile the records and avoid a duplicate payment or asset release.

DocuSign can continue to handle e-signature. None of these alone checks whether a crypto deposit is safe or under control.

Three identity routes, one sensible decision path

01

Data match

Compare supplied identity details with trusted records. Low friction when results are clear.

02

ID + selfie

Ask for a document and live face check when confidence is low or risk is higher.

03

Human review

An analyst resolves exceptions before the applicant may continue.

Proposed route: start with data verification, step up when needed, then manual review. The final policy depends on borrower type and compliance approval.

07 / Business decision

What would make this worth building?

Revenue path A · platform provider

An approved lender pays LendFoundry for setup, software usage, each funded loan and/or servicing. The lender supplies capital and holds the credit exposure. This is the recommended commercial hypothesis to test first.

Revenue path B · lender

The lending business receives borrower interest and permitted fees, but must fund loans and absorb funding, credit, collateral and recovery risk. This would need a separate capital and legal decision.

Economics of one loan: revenue from agreed platform fees or loan interest minus verification, custody, banking, support, capital and expected loss costs = contribution. No real prices, loan sizes or default rates have been approved, so a credible margin estimate requires partner quotes and pilot evidence.

Recommended first release

Include

One U.S. borrower segment and approved state set; one lending partner; Bitcoin collateral; regulated third-party custody; USD funding; identity and financial crime checks; clear agreement; price and LTV monitoring; repayment, warning, controlled liquidation and asset return.

Defer

Multiple assets and chains, stablecoin funding, self-custody, proprietary smart contracts, automatic permissionless liquidation, revolving loans and international rollout.

Decisions before development

Customer + geographyCommercial borrower or consumer? Which U.S. states?Determines the legal program, disclosures, checks and market size.
Business roleSoftware provider, lender, or referral partner?Determines revenue, funding capital, licenses and who bears a loss.
Collateral controlWhich custodian, legal arrangement and no-reuse policy?Determines asset safety, customer trust, costs and liquidation rights.
Loan rulesAmount, rate, term, fees, LTV limits, warning and sale triggers?Determines customer cost, approval volume and loss risk.
Partners + operationsWho provides money, USD transfers, screening and 24×7 response?Determines launch feasibility and whether problems can be handled safely.

What could stop the project?

Regulatory path

The exact lender, custodian, state and money flow might require licenses or a partner structure that is unavailable or uneconomic.

Weak demand or margin

Customers may prefer an incumbent, and partner costs or acquisition costs may exceed what buyers will pay.

Loss or trust event

A fast asset price fall, failed sale, fraud, custody incident or operational error could create loss and damage trust.

Recommendation: validate first. Confirm one customer segment and legal structure, obtain a committed lending partner and indicative vendor prices, test demand with prospective buyers, and simulate a severe price fall. Build a capped pilot only if these checks show a viable customer need, positive contribution and manageable loss exposure.
