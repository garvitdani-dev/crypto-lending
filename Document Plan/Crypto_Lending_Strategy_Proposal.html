01 / The proposal in one page

Extend LendFoundry into a new secured-lending category

An eligible customer pledges cryptocurrency, receives a loan, and repays it over time. The asset is returned after full repayment. If the loan becomes unsafe or defaults, the agreed collateral process protects the lender.

Confirmed from referenceProposed directionDecision requiredIllustrative only

The business case

Customer

Access dollars without first selling a long-term crypto holding.

Lending partner

Offer a collateral-backed product through defined eligibility and risk controls.

LendFoundry

Turn an existing lending platform into reusable infrastructure for a specialist product.

Recommended direction: validate a platform-led, custodial product with one U.S. borrower segment, one lending/capital partner, Bitcoin collateral and USD disbursement. Start with conservative exposure and controlled collateral-sale decisions. Expand only after the first product proves demand, safe operations and positive contribution.

Why it may work

  • The customer problem is simple: liquidity without an immediate asset sale.
  • Collateral can reduce loss exposure when it is securely held and liquid.
  • Most of the lending lifecycle already exists in LendFoundry.
  • Active U.S. offerings show that the category is established.

Why it may not work

  • State-by-state legal requirements may make the model costly.
  • Sharp price falls can outrun warnings and collateral sale.
  • Custody, banking and 24×7 operations add material cost.
  • Known exchanges and specialist lenders already have customer trust.
02 / Crypto lending without jargon

A secured loan where the security moves in value

The loan itself is familiar: application, approval, agreement, funding, repayment and closure. The difference is the collateral. Cryptocurrency trades continuously, so the platform must watch the loan's safety continuously as well.

1 · ApplyRequest funds and identify the asset.
2 · VerifyConfirm customer and product eligibility.
3 · ValueConfirm asset, price and borrowing capacity.
4 · SecurePlace collateral under approved control.
5 · ServiceFund, collect payments and monitor risk.
6 · ResolveReturn collateral or sell under agreed rules.

Follow one customer

Illustrative example
$20,000Bitcoin pledged
$10,000USD loan
50% LTVLoan ÷ collateral value

A customer owns Bitcoin but needs $10,000. Instead of selling, the customer pledges $20,000 of Bitcoin. The starting loan-to-value ratio, or LTV, is 50%. If Bitcoin falls and the collateral becomes worth $13,000 while the balance remains $10,000, LTV rises to about 77%. The loan is now much less protected.

The platform warns the customer and offers two ways to restore safety: add more collateral or repay part of the loan. If the customer does neither and the approved sale threshold is reached, enough collateral may be sold to reduce the debt. After full repayment, the remaining asset is returned.

The customer benefit

The customer keeps market exposure to the asset and receives liquidity now.

The customer trade-off

The customer pays interest and fees, cannot freely use pledged assets, and may experience a forced sale if risk becomes too high.

The product succeeds through understanding, not novelty. A customer must clearly see the loan amount, cost, collateral value, safety cushion, warning level and possible sale outcome before accepting.

05 / What happens when the normal path breaks

The product is defined by its difficult moments

Approval is only one part of the product. Trust is created by predictable behavior when identity is unclear, prices fall, payments fail or a provider is unavailable.

SituationCustomer does not pass eligibility
ResponseDecline or send to documented review; do not create an offer.
Customer outcomeClear status and permissible next step.
SituationPrice is missing or inconsistent
ResponseUse an approved fallback or pause valuation, funding and sale.
Business outcomeNo irreversible action using an untrusted price.
SituationCollateral falls into warning range
ResponseNotify immediately; allow top-up or partial repayment.
Customer outcomeSafety restored if cured; otherwise risk escalates.
SituationSale threshold is reached
ResponseValidate price and authority; sell only the permitted amount; reconcile proceeds.
Financial outcomeDebt reduced or settled; surplus/shortfall handled under terms.
SituationPayment is late
ResponseApply approved grace, notices, fees and default rules.
Open decisionExact cure and default policy must be approved.
SituationCritical provider is unavailable
ResponsePause unsafe actions, use approved fallback and open an incident.
Business outcomeSafe degraded service—not a guessed or duplicated action.
Liquidation is not a button. It is a controlled process involving price evidence, authority to act, an execution venue, customer notices, sale accounting, debt settlement and return of any remaining collateral.
06 / Operating model and partners

LendFoundry coordinates; specialists perform regulated or asset-specific work

The platform remains the central record for the customer and loan. Partners supply capital, protect assets, move dollars and return specialist evidence. Every external result must be tied back to the loan and reconciled.

07 / Market position

The category is real; the position must be specific

Customers can already borrow against crypto through exchanges, specialist lenders and decentralized protocols. LendFoundry should not compete by offering the most assets. It should compete by making the product easier for lending institutions to launch, control and explain.

Market reference Current public model Lesson
Coinbase Eligible U.S. customers can borrow USDC against supported crypto through Morpho on Base; current material describes collateral-based decisions and ongoing LTV risk. Distribution and a familiar interface can hide technical complexity.
Unchained Bitcoin-backed commercial USD loans with collaborative multisignature custody and on-chain visibility. Transparent asset control and a clear customer segment can build trust.
Ledn Bitcoin-backed lending with independent custody, loan-health tools and jurisdiction-dependent availability. Monitoring, proof and simple borrower action are part of the product.
SALT U.S.-oriented crypto-backed loans, multiple payout/collateral choices and risk-protection features. Risk features can differentiate, but also create reserves and contractual cost.
Aave / DeFi Smart contracts manage digital-asset borrowing and automated liquidation. Useful mechanics reference, but a different customer and operating model.

Proposed position: enable a lender to launch a branded, policy-controlled crypto-backed loan without assembling the entire workflow, servicing, evidence and partner ecosystem from scratch.

This position must be tested with qualified lenders. It is an advantage only if buyers value configurable policy, integrated servicing and auditability more than an end-to-end vendor or internal build.

Custody and smart-contract choice

Recommended first model: custodial

A qualified provider controls collateral under contract. The journey is more familiar, customer support is easier and authorized exceptions can be managed. The business accepts custodian dependency and cost.

Possible later model: non-custodial

Smart contracts hold collateral under coded rules. This can improve on-chain transparency but adds wallet friction, code risk, audits, governance and irreversible failure modes.

A smart contract is not required to prove the product. Use one only when a customer, control or economic need is strong enough to justify its additional risk and cost.
08 / Business model and feasibility

The strongest starting model is platform enablement

Platform enablement

Implementation, recurring platform, funded-loan and servicing fees. The lending partner carries capital and loan loss under contract.

Recommended hypothesis.

Lending-program operator

Earn borrower interest and fees, but take materially greater licensing, funding, liquidity and loss exposure.

Referral model

Earn referral revenue from another lender. Faster to market, but with less control and smaller economics.

What makes one loan economically worthwhile?

Contribution = platform and/or interest revenue − capital cost − identity/compliance − custody − price/blockchain data − payments − servicing/support − expected loss − partner share.

No approved interest rate, platform price, loan size, loss rate or volume forecast exists. A credible business case requires vendor quotes, capital terms, buyer willingness to pay, expected manual-review effort and crash-scenario recovery estimates.

Demand test

Will a qualified lending buyer make a paid or binding commitment?

Margin test

Does contracted revenue cover variable cost and stressed losses?

Repeatability test

Can another lender launch without turning the platform into custom consulting?

Regulatory model

Price and liquidity

03

Proceed to a capped MVP if

  • Counsel supports the exact model and states.
  • Required capital, bank and custody partners commit.
  • A qualified buyer demonstrates willingness to pay.
  • Quoted costs and stressed losses support contribution.
  • 24×7 monitoring and incident ownership are funded.

Pause if

  • The legal or partner structure is unavailable or uneconomic.
  • Custody or sale authority remains unclear.
  • No credible commercial commitment exists.
  • Economics remain negative under reasonable stress.
  • Loss cannot be contained during a rapid market fall.
Final recommendation: validate first, then proceed with conditions. The product is a credible extension of LendFoundry and the market category is established. The specific business case is not yet proven. Complete legal, customer, partner and economic validation first. If those gates pass, launch the narrow custodial MVP, measure it closely and expand only from demonstrated demand and safe operating evidence.
