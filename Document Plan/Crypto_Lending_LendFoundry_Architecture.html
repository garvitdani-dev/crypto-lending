1. Executive summary

Crypto-backed lending lets a borrower pledge digital assets and receive liquidity without first selling those assets. The lending decision is driven by both borrower eligibility and collateral risk: ownership/control, provenance, market value, liquidity, volatility, loan-to-value (LTV), custody and enforceable liquidation. Unlike a traditional loan, collateral health must be monitored continuously because crypto markets operate 24×7.

24×7collateral valuation and incident coverage
2 ledgersloan system of record plus custody/on-chain state
3 gatesidentity, wallet/asset risk, collateral control before funding
1 MVPjurisdiction, chain/rail and collateral policy

Recommended direction

Proposed MVP: a U.S.-first, centralized and overcollateralized product in which LendFoundry remains the customer/loan system of record; an approved regulated custodian controls collateral; risk and servicing remain off-chain; funds are disbursed in USD through an approved banking/payment partner; and blockchain data is used for deposit confirmation, screening and reconciliation. Begin with one collateral asset (BTC is the simplest operational boundary), no rehypothecation, conservative configurable LTVs, manual approval for exceptions, and controlled liquidation through the custodian/approved execution venue.

This recommendation intentionally does not require a proprietary smart contract for the first release. A smart-contract vault or protocol integration remains a viable second architecture when self-custody, stablecoin funding or composability is a product requirement. If management requires an EVM on-chain MVP, the document provides that flow and its additional audit, oracle, gas, key-governance and incident-response requirements.

What changes for LendFoundry

  • Keep familiar capabilities: application, consent, customer profile, eligibility, offers, adverse-action/reason handling as applicable, agreements, funding orchestration, servicing, payments, notifications, audit and reporting.
  • Add crypto-native capabilities: wallet/custody-account lifecycle, wallet ownership/control evidence, blockchain analytics, supported asset/network registry, collateral valuation, deposit confirmation, continuous LTV monitoring, top-up and liquidation, on-chain reconciliation, key/transaction policy and 24×7 operations.
  • Do not treat Plaid Identity or a credit bureau as a substitute for CIP/KYC, sanctions screening, wallet screening or collateral control. Each answers a different risk question.

3. Crypto lending fundamentals

Borrower perspective

Pledge approved crypto, receive USD or stablecoin liquidity, pay interest/fees, keep the economic exposure to collateral, and regain collateral after full settlement—subject to liquidation and contractual terms.

Lender perspective

Control eligible collateral with a risk buffer, continuously value it, and sell/transfer collateral under agreed rules if the position becomes unsafe or defaults.

Core mechanics

ConceptMeaningImplementation consequence
CollateralDigital asset securing the obligation.Must be eligible, controlled, segregated/reconciled and liquid enough to exit.
LTVOutstanding exposure ÷ eligible collateral value × 100.Recalculate on price, balance, debt, fee or policy changes.
HaircutDiscount applied to market value for risk.Eligible value may be less than observable market value.
Margin call/top-upBorrower adds collateral or repays debt to restore buffer.Needs notice, deadline, deposit rail and after-hours behavior.
LiquidationCollateral is sold/transferred to reduce exposure.Requires price integrity, authority, venue/liquidity, slippage control and reconciliation.
Oracle/market dataTrusted external price input.Freshness, deviation, fallback and circuit-breaker rules are mandatory.
CustodyControl/safekeeping of private keys and assets.Changes legal structure, operational controls, transaction latency and counterparty risk.
Example only: $20,000 eligible collateral and a $10,000 loan produce 50% LTV. If eligible value falls to $13,000 while exposure stays $10,000, LTV becomes 76.9%. Thresholds in this document are deliberately not prescribed; product/risk/legal owners must approve them per asset.

Positioning opportunity

  • Configurable lender platform: support different lender policies, state footprints, collateral rules and vendors through configuration and adapters.

Service boundary principles

  • Core domain uses internal canonical IDs; provider-specific IDs stay in adapters.
  • All external writes use an idempotency key; all webhooks are signed, replay-protected and safely reprocessable.
  • Events are at-least-once; consumers are idempotent. No business state is advanced solely because an API request returned 200.
  • PII never goes on-chain. On-chain memo fields must not contain application, customer, SSN, email or address data.
  • Every privileged asset movement requires policy evaluation, destination allowlisting and dual control appropriate to risk.

9.2 Optional smart-contract flow

1. Backend completes KYC/AML and product eligibility off-chain. 2. Backend creates a loan intent: terms hash, borrower wallet, asset, amount, expiry, nonce. 3. Authorized signer signs intent; borrower accepts agreement and transaction. 4. Borrower deposits collateral into audited vault contract. 5. Contract validates asset/amount/intent and emits CollateralLocked(loanIdHash,...). 6. Indexer waits finality; backend independently reads chain state and reconciles the event. 7. Oracle feed is checked for value, timestamp, confidence/deviation and chain health. 8. Backend funding gate releases USD, OR contract transfers stablecoin if that is the approved model. 9. Keeper/risk service monitors debt and health; contract enforces only approved on-chain rules. 10. Repayment is posted; contract releases collateral only after authorized proof/state transition. 11. If liquidatable, approved keeper/liquidator executes; proceeds and residual collateral reconcile to LOS.
Critical design rule: “backend calculated it” is not enough when a contract moves value. The contract must validate the authorization, nonce, expiry, replay protection, asset, amount, oracle conditions and permissions that are essential to safe execution. Conversely, KYC/credit details should stay off-chain; commit only a non-identifying reference/hash if truly required.

9.4 Reconciliation

Run near-real-time transaction reconciliation plus a daily full position proof. For every loan compare: LOS locked quantity; custodian available/locked/pending quantity; chain address balance/UTXO set; open withdrawal/deposit; valuation asset and decimals. Breaks create a case, freeze risk-increasing actions, and preserve a complete before/after snapshot. Never “fix” mismatches by silently overwriting one ledger.

Where BitGo fits

BitGo can be evaluated for regulated custody, custody wallets, self-custody wallet infrastructure, transaction policy and APIs. It is not a smart contract, oracle, KYC provider, lender-of-record or LOS. The exact BitGo legal entity, service, wallet type, asset support, key allocation and withdrawal workflow must be fixed in the contract and design. Public BitGo documentation distinguishes custody wallets from self-custody wallets and describes key-control differences.[10]

Smart-contract minimum control set

  • Separate collateral vault from loan/accounting logic where upgrade/lifecycle needs differ.
  • Role-based access, least privilege, multisig/timelock for administration, pause with bounded authority.
  • Reentrancy protection, checks-effects-interactions, safe token handling, decimal normalization and explicit supported-token registry.
  • Oracle freshness/deviation/sequencer controls; fail-safe behavior and risk-reducing actions during pause.
  • Intent signatures with domain separation, nonce, expiry and chain/contract binding.
  • Upgrade policy disclosed and tested; storage-layout checks; emergency governance and key-loss procedure.
  • Independent audit, adversarial testing, invariant/fuzz tests, deployment verification, monitoring and bug bounty before real value.

12. Underwriting, LTV and liquidation

12.1 Calculation model

Gross collateral value = quantity × approved price
Eligible collateral value = gross value × (1 − haircut)
Exposure = principal + accrued interest + due fees + protected liquidation costs (as approved)
LTV = exposure ÷ eligible collateral value

Store every input with units, decimals, source timestamps, policy version and calculation precision. Use fixed-point decimal math; never binary floating-point for financial decisions.

Exchange algorithmTransfer/allocate → staged orders with price/slippage limits → settle → reconcileMore automation/liquidity; exchange and transfer risk.

Third-party liquidatorsPermissionless/allowlisted actors repay debt and receive collateral/bonusScalable protocol pattern; incentive tuning and legal/product complexity.
Consumer treatment and notices: do not assume that overcollateralization removes lending, servicing, disclosure, fair-lending, privacy, collections, bankruptcy or state-law obligations. Legal counsel must determine the nature of the credit, security interest, custody arrangement and liquidation remedy for each borrower type and state.

13. Data, security and operational controls

Data

  • Tokenize/minimize SSN and identity artifacts.
  • Encrypt in transit/at rest; separate keys and duties.
  • Immutable decision and transaction audit trail.
  • Retention/deletion schedule by record type.
  • No PII or document hashes that enable guessing on public chains.

Assets and keys

  • No raw private keys in application services.
  • MPC/HSM or custodian-controlled signing.
  • Destination allowlists and velocity/amount limits.
  • Dual approval and break-glass procedure.
  • Daily position and address reconciliation.

Reliability

  • Two RPC paths and two price sources for critical decisions.
  • Webhook queue, replay and dead-letter handling.
  • 24×7 alerting with named incident commander.
  • Runbooks for oracle, chain, custody, bank and vendor outage.
  • Backup borrower path for risk-reducing actions.

Governance

  • Versioned product/risk policy.
  • Maker-checker for exceptions and movements.
  • Model/vendor performance and bias monitoring.
  • Change approval for assets, contracts and thresholds.
  • Tabletop exercises and vendor exit plans.

14.2 MVP components

  1. Crypto product/asset policy registry.
  2. KYC/fraud orchestration plus AML/sanctions and case queue.
  3. Custodian adapter (wallet/account, deposit, balance, withdrawal, webhooks).
  4. Blockchain observation/indexer and analytics adapter.
  5. Valuation/LTV service with two-source policy and immutable snapshots.
  6. Extended LendFoundry workflow, offer, agreement and conditions-precedent funding gate.
  7. USD payout/repayment and loan subledger integration.

  • Multiple chains/tokens, NFTs, privacy coins, staking/restaking assets or LP tokens.
  • Cross-chain bridges, proprietary stablecoin, permissionless liquidity pool or composable DeFi positions.
  • Automated permissionless liquidation, collateral reuse/rehypothecation, revolving credit and cross-collateral portfolios.
  • Global rollout before U.S. entity/state and product controls are proved.

  • Accounting: fees, accrual, payoff quote, liquidation proceeds, residual return, shortfall, charge-off and daily reconciliation.

18. Glossary

AMLAnti-money-laundering controls, including risk assessment, monitoring, investigation and reporting obligations as applicable.
CIP/KYCCustomer identification / know-your-customer processes used to establish and verify identity.
CustodialA custodian controls private keys/assets under an agreed legal arrangement.
DeFiBlockchain-based financial protocols generally executed through smart contracts.
FinalityConfidence that a blockchain transaction will not be reversed; policy varies by network and value.
HaircutRisk discount applied to collateral market value.
LTVLoan/exposure divided by eligible collateral value.
MPCMulti-party computation, a key-management/signing technique distributing control.
OracleMechanism delivering external data, such as prices, to an application or smart contract.
RehypothecationReuse of pledged collateral for another transaction or obligation.
RPCInterface used to query a blockchain node and submit transactions.
Smart contractCode deployed to a blockchain that changes on-chain state under defined rules.
StablecoinToken designed to track a reference asset such as the U.S. dollar; it still carries issuer, reserve, freeze, chain and depeg risk.
UTXOBitcoin-style unspent transaction output used to represent spendable value.
